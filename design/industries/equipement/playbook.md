# equipement — playbook (READ by the composer AND the filler)

> The **STRUCTURE and the concept come from HERE**. The trade decides the information architecture; fonts + archetype = flavour.

## What this trade sells
**Professional equipment** (machines, gear, lines, technical installations for industry/businesses).
A B2B client, a rational and technical purchase. A wide ticket (€5k-500k). The client buys **performance, reliability,
after-sales service** and a partner who understands their need. Often a range + bespoke/integration + recurring
maintenance.

## Concept & angle
**The equipment partner**: the right machine for the right process, installed and maintained. A serious, competent,
solution-oriented industrial tone. Sell reliability, productivity and after-sales service (the recurring part). Set
it apart through trade expertise and support, not just the catalogue.

## STRUCTURE — the trade's information architecture (≠ outillage: here heavy/installed B2B equipment, not hand tools)
1. **Hero**: a strong installation/machine + promise ("Professional equipment for <sector>") + CTA **"Request a
   quote / advice"**.
2. **Ranges & solutions**: equipment families + sectors served (the visitor finds their need).
3. **Bespoke / integration**: study → installation → commissioning (when it applies to the client).
4. **Projects / installations**: equipment in place at clients' sites — the proof of reliability, shown big.
5. **Service & after-sales**: maintenance, parts, repairs, contracts (the recurring part — highlight it).
6. **References & brands**: clients, brands distributed/manufactured, certifications — a pledge of B2B seriousness.
7. **Final CTA**: "Request a quote" / "Get a call back from an expert".

## Mood
Industrial-professional, technical and crisp. A sturdy grotesque (Suisse Intl, GT America, Monument Grotesk, Fakt) ×
mono for specs. A steel/technical grey palette + 1 industrial accent. Sober motion, crisp reveals, readable data.

## Avoid
A "general public/lifestyle" tone. Vague pages with no specs or references (the professional buyer wants concrete
facts). Hiding the after-sales service (it is a strong argument). Confusing it with outillage (consumables/hand tools)
or job-site equipment.

## Photos
Real equipment/installations in the workshop and at clients' sites, crisp views, technical details.
A real installation > 3D renders or stock images.
